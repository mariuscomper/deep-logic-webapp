# Brief: what to take back in time

**One sentence.** The best thing to carry back in time is the one the era can copy, not the one that impresses it.

**What the data said.** Twenty objects, scored on four dimensions (Need, Copy, Trust, Ripple) at their best landing among nine historical windows. Ordering by Copy alone reproduces the full ranking at ρ = 0.90. Ordering by Trust alone (how fast people believe you) does not (ρ ≈ 0 on the author's scores, negative on each rater's). The year of the best landing is unrelated to the score (ρ ≈ −0.07): going further back does not help.

**How it was tested (before any design).**

1. Weights: 5,000 seeded random weightings. The potato, the rehydration salts and the stamp set stay in the top five in over 90% of draws; the middle of the list is soft.
2. A second body of scores: three raters in separate contexts, given only a neutral one-line description of each object, the nine windows and the four definitions, and told not to read files. Their rankings agree with the author's at ρ 0.76 to 0.81. They agree with each other more (0.89 to 0.97), so this is a consistency check by the same model family, not independent evidence. The author's scores were fixed before the raters ran and were not edited afterwards.
3. Fact-check: four fresh reviewers went through every checkable claim in the first draft and found about fifty errors, all fixed (neodymium magnets and the lodestone claim, the coffee and water myth, Mainz having no crown, Magellan's survivors, the ORS sachet size, the Broad Street counts and others). A second pass re-checked the rewritten text and every source link.

**What failed.** The first idea was a ranking by the author's judgement alone. The raters disagree most about the slide rule (author 4th, raters 14th: they scored its Need at 3 or 4), ether (8th vs 2nd) and WD-40 (15th vs last). All three would land the antibiotic course in the Victorian age, not at Alexander's deathbed, and the lighters in the Classical world. The page prints these gaps beside the ranking. The expectation that the most persuasive objects would also be the most useful was wrong.

**The picture.** The model's own surface: 20 objects by 9 eras, one cell per landing, lit by Delta. Rows are anonymous until the reader lands them; a row the reader has touched goes dark except where they landed, so a retry stays a guess. At the end the surface sorts into the ranking.

**The reader's own case.** Their object, their era, their score, a boarding pass drawn from the same data, and a link whose preview is that boarding pass (one static page and one image per landing, 180 per edition).

**Editions.** English at the root (this site's other pages are English); Romanian beside it, drafted in separate passes under one terminology brief and reviewed as a whole.

**Must not claim.** That the scores are measurements; that the raters are independent of the author's model family; that counterfactual history can be settled by data.

**Left open (judgement).** The weights; the nine windows (coarse on purpose); which landing is "best" for objects where the raters disagree.
